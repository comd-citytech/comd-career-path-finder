Intro

The job our degree is named for is shrinking, slowly. Every other job it points at is not. That is the whole story of this issue, and it is better news than it sounds.

Graphic design employment is projected to fall two percent through 2035. The government's own explanation is that AI tools let one designer do what two did. The same sentence shows up in the outlook for writers and for illustrators.

That is a job title shrinking, not a field. Communication design is the skill. The titles in here are nine careers that skill opens, and five of them are growing. The best-paying ones pair it with a second thing: code, motion, research, or the ability to run a project.

Most of what you need for those second things is already in the course catalog. Several courses feed three careers at once. The pages that follow show which, so that four skills slots can open more than one door.

Read the cards for the work. Read the data spread for the money. Read the combinations for what to register for in April.

Turn a message into something people see: logos, layouts, packaging, social posts, decks, web graphics. The Ladder

Most design careers climb the same four steps, and pay roughly doubles from the bottom to the top.

The government says most art directors spent five or more years as designers, illustrators, editors or photographers first. Robert Half asks for five to eight.

Years 0–2

Junior & production designer

$47–65K

You execute. Someone else has the idea; you build the files and learn how work ships. Your portfolio got you hired. Speed and reliability get you promoted.

Years 2–5

Designer, then senior

$53–81K

You own the concept, present it, and specialize. Digital, presentation and visual design pay $13K to $28K more. The ladder forks here toward UX and front-end.

Years 5–8

Art director

$85–124K

You decide what the work looks like and direct the people who make it. $114,850 median; top ten percent over $213,000. The job is mostly meetings.

Years 8–15

Creative director

$103–166K

You own the creative output of an agency, brand or studio. Sixty-two percent at this level are self-employed, which often means a studio of their own.

In advertising, junior art director exists at the first step: an agency pairs you with a junior copywriter from day one. It pays like a junior designer, but you skip a step if the work is good.

Straight talk

The job called graphic designer is shrinking. The field is not.

AI tools let one designer do what two did. That is the government's own explanation, and it shows up in every design-adjacent job here.

It does not mean there are no jobs. It means the jobs go to people who bring something beyond layout. New York pays twenty-five to forty percent above national for most of this work and has more openings in advertising, publishing, media, museums and tech than anywhere except Los Angeles and the Bay Area. You picked the right place to study this.

Find your closest job in the Path Finder
  • Pick a second skill early. Design is the skill you are already building. Every career here over $90,000 stacks one more on top: code, motion, research, or production. Choose yours by your third semester.
  • Learn the tools employers list. Across nine jobs: Creative Cloud, Figma, PowerPoint and Keynote, After Effects, HTML and CSS. Two of those are easy to self-teach and most of your competition will not bother.
  • Use AI tools and say so. Employers list them as required skills. Show a workflow that uses them well.
  • Search the titles, not the job. Product designer, digital designer, brand designer, production artist, content designer, motion designer, video editor, junior art director.
Straight Talk
Straight talk

The ladder still works. It may not keep this shape.

The four steps on the previous page are what the field looks like today, and what employers still hire against. Worth knowing: a lot of people in the industry think AI is about to change the climb itself.

The argument runs like this. Treat it as a forecast, not a finding.

Step 1 · today you execute

You may direct the AI instead

Comps, resizes and production work are what these tools do fastest. The worry is that the work which used to teach you the craft is the first to go, so juniors judge and fix output rather than make it from nothing.

Step 2 · today you own the concept

You may curate more than you draw

Less time on the twentieth version, more on deciding which of twenty is right and why. The scarce skill becomes taste you can defend out loud.

Step 3 · today you direct the look

Strategy may crowd out craft

If production gets cheap, art direction tilts further toward brand story, research and pointing the exploration somewhere useful.

Step 4 · today you own the output

The top step may move closer

If the steps below compress, the climb could be shorter and more competitive at once: fewer seats, reached sooner, won on judgment rather than years served.

What is actually measured

Across every occupation, not design alone, Indeed Hiring Lab found entry-level postings down 7.5 percent year over year in May 2026 while senior-level postings rose 14.7 percent. Indeed lists AI as one possible cause among several, alongside remote work and interest rates. The Bureau of Labor Statistics does name AI directly: it is the reason given for graphic design shrinking two percent. Nobody has published a reliable count of junior design roles lost to AI, so treat any single dramatic percentage you see online with suspicion.

  • Do not wait for step one to teach you. If entry-level work is the part most exposed, build the judgment on your own: real projects, real constraints, real critique.
  • Learn to defend a choice. Every version of this forecast agrees on one thing. The person who can say why this one and not that one is the person who keeps moving.
  • None of this is settled. It is a forecast from people with opinions, not a measurement. Plan for the ladder on the previous page, and stay ready for this one.
